Sharing

There are two ways to share: with people who have an account, and with anyone through a link.

On this page
  1. Share with colleagues
  2. Restricted folders
  3. Share with a link
  4. Your links
  5. For administrators

Share with colleagues

Right-click a folder or file and choose Share with…. Type a username or group, choose what they may do, and optionally a date when the access ends, then click Add. Someone whose own access here ends on a date can't give themselves (or a group of theirs, or everyone) access that lasts longer.

The item appears under Shared with me in their menu, and they are told under the bell (and by email, if it is set up): see Notifications.

ViewerBrowse, preview and download

Items found in a folder on the server count as someone else's, and a folder they made can't be moved or deleted while it holds anyone else's items. No share links, and no managing members

Restricted folders

Keep a small team's HR or Contracts material in the department's folder tree without giving every space member access. A space owner opens the folder's Share with… dialog and chooses Restrict folder. The dialog lists the inherited grants that will stop and asks for confirmation. Existing folders remain unrestricted until you choose this.

  • Grants above the folder stop at its boundary. Grants on it and below it still apply, until another restricted folder stops them. A directly shared descendant appears under Shared with me without revealing folders above it that the person cannot open.
  • Space owners retain access. Administrators retain access in team and company spaces; other people's personal spaces stay private. Managers need an explicit grant on the restricted folder or a descendant. Only space owners and these administrators can turn restrictions on or off.
  • Hidden names, files, sizes and records are omitted from lists, search, tags, Recent, Favorites, history, notifications and the trash. Visible usage excludes inaccessible files; the actual space quota still counts all stored files. WebDAV and SFTP follow the same access rules.
  • A parent folder's ZIP download includes only visible items. Copying, compressing, moving or deleting a whole parent is refused when it would include descendants the person cannot access or change. Moving a restricted folder retains its restriction; copying it retains the restriction but does not copy member grants. Ordinary descendants moved out inherit their destination's grants.
  • A share link, S3 bucket or FTP receive scope published above a restricted folder cannot cross its boundary, even if its creator is an owner or administrator. Explicitly publish the restricted folder itself to allow that scope; nested restricted folders still remain outside it.

Choose Restore inherited access to allow the parent grants again, after checking the confirmation. Space roots and folders in the trash cannot be toggled. Access is checked again when an upload or other pending change is committed. Saved records whose old target can no longer be verified are protected conservatively; owners and administrators can still read the management records they are entitled to see.

Share with a link

Right-click an item and choose Create share link, or select it and click Create share link on the toolbar. Anyone with the link can view and download, without an account, unless you choose otherwise.

  • Expiration: the link stops working after 1, 7, 30 or 90 days, or never.
  • Password: visitors must enter it before they see anything, and again after a day. It must be as long as account passwords (Control panel › General). After ten wrong passwords for a link, each further try has to wait longer, from any address. Send the password separately from the link.
  • Download limit: the link stops working after this many downloads. A download that is resumed, or a video that is skipped through, counts once; each file of a folder counts on its own.
  • Visitors can: whether they may download, and for a folder, whether they may upload files. See below.

To change a link's password, expiry, download limit or what visitors can do, click Edit link next to it. The address stays the same. After a new password, people who opened the link before must enter the new one.

Receive files with a link

To collect files, for example from customers, create a link to a folder and tick Upload files. Visitors then get an Upload button and can drag files onto the folder.

  • Tick Only upload as well, and visitors see nothing of what is in the folder and can't download anything; they only get a box to send files into the folder itself. A dropped folder arrives as its files, and when a name is taken the file is kept under a new one without telling the visitor.
  • Uploaded files belong to you, count toward the space's size, and follow the usual rules for names and the upload size limit. A file whose name is taken gets a number, like report (1).pdf.
  • Each upload is recorded in the activity log, under your name with Through share link, and in the link's access log. You get a notification when files arrive (Notifications).
  • Uploads only work while you can still change the folder. When you lose that permission, or the folder is moved out of the shared folder, uploads through the link stop.
  • Unfinished uploads through one link can hold up to 20 GB at a time. An upload through a link that receives nothing for a day is dropped, and the visitor sends that file again.
  • An unfinished upload can only be continued from the browser that started it. Other visitors of the same link can't see, continue or cancel it.

Links for viewing only

Untick Download and visitors can only preview files: the download buttons and the ZIP download are gone, and the server refuses downloads through the link.

This is a deterrent, not a protection. To show a preview, the whole file is still sent to the visitor's browser, and someone determined can save it from there, or take a screenshot. Don't rely on it for files that must not leave the company.

Your links

My share links in the menu lists every link you created, with its expiry date and how often it was used. Select a link to copy, edit or delete it; Delete link stops it working right away.

Switch the list to All links I can manage to also see links other people made in spaces you manage, and on files you own. You can edit and delete those links too, and see who opened them. The share dialog and the details pane of an item show the same links.

Who can make links in a team space or All files is chosen by its managers, at the bottom of the space's Members dialog: editors, managers and owners (the default), only managers and owners, or nobody. Members can still share items with people (Share with…). Links made by people the choice no longer allows stop working, and work again if it is changed back; the dialog says how many first. In My files, you always decide.

A link also stops working when its creator loses access to the item or the permission to share, for example when they leave a team space, when the space no longer lets them make links, and when their account is disabled. Deleting an account deletes the links it created.

For administrators

Control panel › All share links lists every public link, in every space, with who created it. Filter by space, by creator, and by whether the link still works, then edit or delete links, or open their access log. Links in other people's personal spaces can only be deleted, and their address and item aren't shown (What administrators can see).

Under Control panel › General › Share links, set the rules for links:

  • Allow public share links: when off, no one can create links, and every existing link stops working until you turn it back on. The links aren't deleted.
  • Require a password: new links must have a password, and passwords can't be removed.
  • Longest expiry: new and changed links must expire within this many days.

The last two rules apply to links created or changed afterwards. Links created earlier keep working as they are; find them in All share links and edit or delete them.

Spaces

A space is a drive with its own members and size limit.

  • My files: your personal space. Only you can see it, unless you share something from it.
  • All files: the company-wide space. An administrator can give it another name.
  • Team spaces: for a department or project. Managers add and remove members.

A folder can also be published as an S3 bucket for programs. Administrators, and space managers allowed by the S3 setting, create folder-specific keys. Personal spaces and spaces whose link makers are Nobody cannot be published.

For scanners and other devices, publish an FTP receive folder with a separate, once-shown device password. Administrators and allowed space managers manage its permissions and expiry; guest accounts and personal spaces cannot publish. Listing and downloading start disabled. Stopping or deleting credentials keeps the files.

Guest access and sharing policy

Under Control panel › General › Guest access and sharing, set the maximum number of days for access given to guests (90 by default). Every new or updated direct grant to a guest, or grant to a group containing a guest, needs an end date within this limit, even when an administrator gives it. Choose Editor, Contributor or Viewer. Changing the setting does not rewrite existing end dates. Expired access stops in the browser, WebDAV and SFTP.

Share only within your own groups limits new or updated access grants: a user must share a current group with you, and a target group must currently include you. The picker follows this policy. Administrators are exempt. Existing grants to unrelated people remain valid; remove them explicitly if they should end. This setting governs account and group access, rather than changing existing public links.
